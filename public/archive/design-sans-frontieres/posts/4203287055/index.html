i was invited to give lecture at the hong kong


I was invited to give lecture at the Hong Kong University (HKU) in Shanghai for the Undergraduate students at the Faculty of Architecture. The broad topic for the study is categorized under the subject of ’Contemporary Design in China’ but I was specifically invited to offer an alternative perspective from different type of design discipline; game design. 

To specifically talk about games design and its whole nine yards in front of people who are not your usual crowd was indeed a challenge. For one, they won’t know nor care about all those fancy lingo that we love to throw around when talking about games, UX or Ix Design. Heck, I was lucky enough to know that at least most of the students actually play games (at some point), which helped mapping out my audience.

I opted to talk about Ix (Interaction) Design and UX (User Experience) in Games and how some lessons in developing games are actually applicable in real life. On how a great Ix Design would help lessen the frustration when playing a game. Simply put, every player wants to be able to navigate the game easily, to know where things are so they can enjoy playing the game and not wasting time learning how to use the tool to play the game. This, of course, will lead to what we call User Experience in games, or the level of enjoyment that people experience when playing a game. 

As said by Raph Kaster, “Fun from games arises out of mastery. It arises out of comprehension. It is the act of solving puzzles that makes games fun. With games, learning is the drug.” (A Theory of Fun for Game Design).
